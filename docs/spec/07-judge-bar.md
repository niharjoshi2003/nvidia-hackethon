# What judges have been rewarding

This event has not announced winners. Deadline is 30 October 2026. The notes below are from nearby 2026 events, used so we aim the demo at the same kind of result.

## This event’s four scores

Technological implementation, design, potential impact, quality of the idea. Same four labels GitLab used in February–March 2026. Their winners were agents that finished a job inside an existing workflow. Judges called out the demo and the UX. A chatbot did not place.

## Nebius, August 2026

The Serverless AI Builders Challenge (35 qualified projects) scored technical implementation, reproducibility, how deeply the Nebius product was used, real usefulness, and originality. The top three each had a measurement:

- 1st, MediSimplifier. Rewrote hospital discharge notes without dropping a clinical fact, and reported that two LLM judges barely agreed.
- 2nd, a dashboard that compares models on **your** data by quality, latency, and cost. Public leaderboards had never seen the task.
- 3rd, Balagan. Injected faults, including a lying agent, into a multi-agent system and measured the token cost of surviving them.

Our cousin is 2nd place: grade against a private rubric. The rubric is the sixty topic. The new part is that the answer is spoken, and the miss changes the next session. The week-3 agreement count is our version of their measurement.

## NVIDIA, April 2026

At Nemotron Developer Days in Seoul, the overall winner used Nemotron 3 Super to build a dataset for model compression. Other track winners were a production agent team and a video-safety model. The pattern was a problem NVIDIA already talks about, with the model doing the hard step. Our hard step is the miss, not transcription.

## What that means for us

Ship the grade and the second session. Put one honest count in the README. Leave the rest of sixty as the finished shell around that result.

A flashcard generator, a face-video demo, or a miss that only says “be more specific” will look like the entries these judges skip.
